The staircase

Same approach, a much bigger scene. The thirty-two steps and their ninety-degree turn, the hall around them, the shelves, the props and the walk up are all written as scripts. Blender renders the climb once; the website you are scrolling plays it back.

Under the hood

How it works

The fog is not in the render.
Blender bakes a second, invisible pass per frame: distance from the camera, half a metre to forty. The browser reads it in a WebGL shader and builds the haze live, so it keeps drifting when you stop scrolling.
The surfaces are photographed.
Stone, weathered timber and book cloth are scanned CC0 materials from Poly Haven, with colour, roughness and normal maps, so the light finds real grain to catch.
The camera has no timeline of its own.
It rides the same walking line the stairs were generated from, which is how it takes the turn without being aimed. Its 240 frames are mapped straight onto how far down the page you are.

What Can I Build with Claude?

I’ve played around with Blender before, but then I found out Claude can help you model objects and even entire scenes… obviously, I had to try it myself. And because a static 3D scene wasn’t enough, I made it interactive too. So you can move through it by scrolling. The goal of this project was to learn, experiment, and see what I’m capable of creating together with Claude. Basically, the perfect kind of project for my playground.
